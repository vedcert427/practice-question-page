ML Methods & TypologiesMedium

A monitoring rule flags a retail customer who has made four separate cash deposits in one week at the same branch — $8,500, $9,100, $8,900 and $9,400 — each just below the $10,000 currency transaction reporting threshold. What is the most appropriate first action?

  1. A. File a suspicious activity report immediately — the pattern is obvious
  2. B. Review the account's historical activity and customer profile to establish whether the pattern is explainable before escalating
  3. C. Contact the customer to ask why they keep depositing just below the threshold
  4. D. Close the account immediately to mitigate the risk
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct answer: B

Structuring (smurfing) is a red flag, but a SAR is a fact-based filing: the first step is to review the account's normal activity, the customer's profile (a contractor with cash income may legitimately deposit near thresholds), and any prior alerts. Acting without that review risks a weak or erroneous filing.

  • A — Premature. Filing without completing the fact-finding step can produce inaccurate reports.
  • C — Contacting the customer about the pattern risks tipping off, which is prohibited in many jurisdictions.
  • D — Disproportionate. Exiting a relationship is a decision for senior management after investigation, not a first response to an alert.

Concepts: structuring (BSA 31 CFR 1010.314(e)); tipping-off prohibitions (FATF Rec. 20 / local SAR law). Source: ACAMS CAMS exam outline — ML methods & typologies.

AML Laws & RegulationsMedium

A U.S. bank is asked to open a correspondent account for a foreign bank that also offers payable-through account (PTA) services to that foreign bank's own customers. Under the USA PATRIOT Act, what must the U.S. bank do before providing PTA services?

  1. A. Obtain a list of the account holders at the foreign bank who will use the PTA services
  2. B. Obtain a list of all politically exposed persons who own the correspondent bank
  3. C. Require the foreign bank to maintain a physical branch in the United States
  4. D. Obtain the foreign bank's complete AML training program
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct answer: A

Section 313 of the USA PATRIOT Act imposes special due diligence on PTA relationships. Because PTA holders effectively bank through the U.S. bank without a direct account there, the U.S. bank must identify those individuals — including a list of account holders who will use the services, the foreign bank's identity and ownership, verification that the foreign bank is not a shell bank, and a designated U.S. contact for service of legal process.

  • B — PEP status is relevant to CDD generally, but the PTA-specific obligation is the account-holder list.
  • C — No such requirement exists; correspondence relationships routinely involve banks with no U.S. branch.
  • D — The U.S. bank assesses the adequacy of the foreign bank's AML program, but obtaining the full training program is not a legal requirement.

Source: USA PATRIOT Act §313 (18 U.S.C. §5318(i)); Wolfsberg Principles on correspondent banking. ACAMS CAMS outline — laws & regulations.

AML Programs & CDDHard

During an annual review, a private bank discovers that a long-standing wealth-management client has recently become a senior minister in a government assessed as high-risk for corruption. What is the most appropriate next step?

  1. A. Exit the relationship immediately — the client's new status makes it non-compliant
  2. B. Escalate to senior management and conduct enhanced due diligence, including source of wealth and source of funds, before continuing the relationship
  3. C. Continue the relationship unchanged — the client relationship predates the new status
  4. D. Move the client to a standard retail product to reduce oversight requirements
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct answer: B

Existing clients who become politically exposed persons after the relationship begins are not automatically disqualified — but the relationship requires senior-management approval, enhanced due diligence to establish source of wealth and source of funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring. Long tenure does not remove the PEP obligation, and exiting without assessment is disproportionate.

  • A — Automatic exit is not required; FATF Rec. 12 expects a risk-based decision with EDD, not a blanket refusal.
  • C — PEP status triggers EDD regardless of when the relationship began; "business as usual" is a compliance failure.
  • D — Downgrading the product does not reduce the legal standard; it merely hides the risk, which regulators treat worse than the original issue.

Source: FATF Recommendation 12 (PEPs); Basel Committee AML/CFT guidance for private banking. ACAMS CAMS outline — AML/BSA programs.

SanctionsMedium

A sanctions screening alert matches a customer, "Al-Hussein Trading LLC," against a list entry for "Al Hussein Trading." Before resolving the alert, what is the analyst's best next step?

  1. A. Clear the alert — the names are different, so it is clearly a false positive
  2. B. Compare identifying attributes (registration numbers, addresses, ownership, dates of birth/incorporation) with the list entry to determine whether they refer to the same entity
  3. C. Block the account immediately as a precaution while the match is reviewed
  4. D. Ask the customer to submit a signed declaration that they are not the listed entity
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct answer: B

Match resolution is an attribute-comparison exercise: name similarity alone is neither sufficient to block nor sufficient to clear. The analyst should line up registration numbers, addresses, ownership and dates against the list entry, and only then resolve the match — as a true or false positive — with a documented rationale.

  • A — "Clearly" is the trap: near-name matches are exactly where true positives hide.
  • C — Precautionary blocking before resolution risks harming a legitimate customer and, depending on jurisdiction, may itself create obligations (and tipping-off exposure).
  • D — A customer's self-declaration is not a basis for match resolution; the institution must do its own analysis.

Source: OFAC guidance on sanctions compliance / match resolution practices; Wolfsberg sanctions screening principles. ACAMS CAMS outline — international & industry standards.

ML Methods & TypologiesMedium

A textile manufacturer in Country X purchases industrial equipment from a supplier in Country Y with which it shares common ownership. The invoice is roughly three times the market value of comparable equipment, with no quality difference to justify it. Which trade-based money laundering technique does this most likely represent?

  1. A. Over-invoicing, used to move value out of Country X to a connected party abroad
  2. B. Under-invoicing, used to move value into Country X
  3. C. Phantom shipment of goods that were never produced
  4. D. Double invoicing, where goods are billed twice
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct answer: A

In over-invoicing, the importer pays more than the goods are worth; the excess flows to the connected foreign seller, moving value out of the importing country — here, out of X into Y. Shared ownership is the classic red flag that the "arm's length" price is a value-transfer mechanism.

  • B — Under-invoicing declares values below market (often to shift value inward or evade duties); the invoice here is above market.
  • C — Phantom shipments involve no goods at all; equipment was actually purchased.
  • D — Double invoicing bills for the same goods twice; here one invoice is simply inflated.

Source: FATF Trade-Based Money Laundering typologies (over/under-invoicing); ACAMS TBLM materials. Outline — ML methods & typologies.

    AML Programs & CDDEasy

    A junior compliance analyst is preparing a suspicious activity report. While waiting to file, the analyst's parent asks — as a favor — whether the bank has "filed anything" on a mutual acquaintance. What is the most appropriate response?

    1. A. Explain that SAR matters are confidential and that even confirming whether a report was filed is not permitted
    2. B. Confirm that a report was filed, but share no details
    3. C. Confirm only that the acquaintance is a customer of the bank
    4. D. Refer the question to the public-relations team
    Show Answer & Explanation

    Correct answer: A

    SAR confidentiality is strict: the existence of a filing is itself protected information in most jurisdictions, and disclosing it — even to family, even without details — can constitute tipping off and expose both the filing and the institution. The correct behavior is a flat "I can't discuss that" with no confirmation of any kind.

    • B — Confirming the filing leaks the very information the confidentiality rule protects.
    • C — Customer status can be sensitive too (e.g., under investigation) and answering opens the door to further probing.
    • D — PR has no authority over SAR confidentiality and routing the question outward increases exposure.

    Source: SAR/STR confidentiality and tipping-off provisions (e.g., BSA §5318(g)(2); FATF Rec. 20). Outline — AML/BSA programs.

    AML Programs & CDDMedium

    When verifying a new corporate customer's registered address during CDD, which source provides the strongest independent, verifiable information?

    1. A. The address published on the customer's website
    2. B. A certificate of incorporation signed by the customer
    3. C. The entry in the government company registry where the entity is incorporated
    4. D. A written reference from one of the customer's existing clients
    Show Answer & Explanation

    Correct answer: C

    CDD standards distinguish customer-provided information from independent, reliable sources. A government registry is authoritative, maintained by a state body, and not produced by the customer for this purpose — that independence is what makes it verifiable. Customer-supplied documents and self-published materials can be altered; a third-party reference is opinion, not verification.

    • A — Self-published; websites are controlled by the customer and trivially changeable.
    • B — Certificates can be forged, and here they are presented by the customer themselves.
    • D — A client's reference confirms business relationships, not corporate facts, and is not independent of the transaction.

    Source: FATF Recommendations 10–11 (CDD — reliable, independent source documents); Basel Committee CDD principles. Outline — AML/BSA programs.

    AML Programs & CDDMedium

    A financial institution decides to enter a new, higher-risk market segment: private banking for clients of non-profit organizations. Under a risk-based approach, what should the institution do first?

    1. A. Apply its existing standard controls to the new segment to maintain consistency
    2. B. Update the institution-wide risk assessment and adjust customer acceptance, controls, and monitoring proportionate to the new risks identified
    3. C. Decline to onboard any clients in the new segment until regulators approve the entry
    4. D. Outsource the entire segment to a third party to transfer the risk
    Show Answer & Explanation

    Correct answer: B

    The risk-based approach means controls follow the risk assessment, not the other way around. Entering a new segment is a trigger to reassess — product, customer, geography and delivery-channel risk — and then right-size customer acceptance criteria, CDD intensity, transaction monitoring, and reporting thresholds. Uniform controls (A) are the anti-pattern regulators specifically call out.

    • A — One-size-fits-all controls under-serve high risk and over-serve low risk; that is precisely what "risk-based" means to avoid.
    • C — Market entry is a business decision subject to the institution's own risk appetite; it does not require regulator pre-approval.
    • D — Outsourcing transfers execution, not accountability; the institution retains full responsibility for the segment's AML controls.

    Source: FATF Rec. 1 & 10 (risk-based approach); ACAMS risk-assessment guidance. Outline — AML/BSA programs.

    What the CAMS Exam Covers

    The five exam domains from the ACAMS exam outline, with approximate weightings and the #1 trap test-takers hit in each. (Approximate — treat the official ACAMS outline as authoritative.)

    Domain 1 · AML & Sanctions Laws, Regulations & Industry Initiatives (~20%)
    • Know which jurisdiction's rule applies in each scenario — U.S. (BSA/PATRIOT), EU, FATF — the exam mixes them deliberately.
    • Sector rules (banks vs. casinos vs. virtual-asset providers) change the obligations; note who the "financial institution" is before answering.
    Domain 2 · ML & Terrorism-Financing Methods & Typologies (~20%)
    • Scenario questions recycle the same typologies (structuring, TBLM, shell companies, trade invoicing) in new stories — learn the pattern, not the story.
    • "Which is the most likely indicator" questions reward you for ranking red flags by strength, not just recognizing one.
    Domain 3 · International & Industry Standards for AML/PTF (~15%)
    • FATF Recommendations, Wolfsberg Principles, Basel Committee papers — the exam tests application (what the standard says an institution must do), not recitation.
    • Distinguish minimum standards (FATF) from best practice (Wolfsberg) when options are close.
    Domain 4 · AML/BSA Compliance Programs (~30%)
    • The heaviest domain: program design, risk assessment, CDD/EDD, monitoring, SARs, training, governance.
    • Default to "the most prudent, documented, risk-based first step" — the exam rarely rewards aggressive or informal answers.
    Domain 5 · AML Auditing (~15%)
    • Know the auditor's limits: the auditor validates remediation; management remediates. Role-confusion is the classic trap.
    • Audit-sampling and materiality questions test whether you understand what an opinion covers.

    AML Glossary — Terms That Appear on the Exam

    Structuring (smurfing)
    Splitting transactions below reporting thresholds to avoid currency-transaction reporting — itself a red flag regardless of intent.
    SAR / STR
    Suspicious Activity Report (U.S.) / Suspicious Transaction Report (elsewhere). Confidential; filing is mandatory, not optional, when suspicion exists.
    PEP
    Politically Exposed Person — domestic, foreign, or international-organization. Triggers senior-management approval, EDD, and source-of-wealth/funds verification.
    CDD / EDD
    Customer / Enhanced Due Diligence. EDD adds independent verification, source-of-funds checks, and higher monitoring intensity for higher-risk customers.
    Correspondent banking
    Bank-to-bank relationships where the respondent serves the correspondent's customers. PTA (payable-through) arrangements carry the heaviest §313 due diligence.
    Trade-based ML (TBLM)
    Moving value via trade — over/under-invoicing, phantom shipments, double invoicing — exploiting the difficulty of verifying physical goods across borders.
    Sanctions
    Comprehensive (whole jurisdiction), sectoral (specific industries), or targeted (specific persons/entities). Freezing vs. licensing distinctions matter on the exam.
    FIU
    Financial Intelligence Unit — receives and analyzes SARs and shares information internationally (e.g., via the Egmont Group).

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How hard is the CAMS exam, really?

    Rigorous and broad — it mixes five domains and rewards depth in domain 4 (compliance programs). ACAMS does not publish an official overall pass rate, so treat vendor claims like "99% pass rate" with skepticism. Realistic preparation for most professionals: 6–12 weeks of focused study.

    Do I need a prerequisite to sit the exam?

    No formal prerequisite. ACAMS recommends relevant experience or coursework in AML/compliance, and many candidates hold an ACAMS membership (which also reduces the exam fee). You can book through Pearson VUE year-round.

    How long is the certification valid?

    Three years. Renewal requires 30 CPEs (Continuing Professional Education) within the cycle, plus the renewal fee. CPEs must be relevant to AML and approved through ACAMS or an approved provider.

    Do I need to buy a vendor prep course?

    No. The official ACAMS materials (exam outline, reference materials, ACAMS' own practice questions) plus a serious independent question bank are sufficient. What you do need is volume: most successful candidates answer 800–1,500 practice questions under time pressure before exam day.
